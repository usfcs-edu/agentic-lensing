\subtitle{The capstone of the parity program: a pre-registered, truth-anchored test of
whether any machine system grades DESI-resolution lens candidates at trained-human
level --- what the human baseline turns out to be worth against truth, which machine
systems survived a frozen evidence gate, and what today's fourteen refutations can and
cannot decide}

\begin{abstract}
The companion human-baseline report made ``as good as a human grader'' a number for the
DESI Legacy Surveys strong-lens search \citep{huang2020decals,huang2021desi,
storfer2024dr9,inchausti2025}: two trained graders agree at $\qwk=0.42$, an individual
grader matches the published consensus at $0.776$, an independent expert team at
$0.29$, and confirmation purity among decided follow-ups is high and flat in grade
($95.2/92.0/90.9\%$). This report is the program's capstone: it builds the apparatus to
decide whether a machine meets those targets, and runs the comparison. The design is
two-endpoint --- E2 (primary, truth-referenced): machine score and human ordinal grade
as \emph{paired} predictors of confirmed-vs-refuted follow-up truth; E1 (secondary):
agreement with the consensus --- executed with a leak firewall and a frozen,
score-once evidence gate. Three findings frame the result. \emph{First}, the human
grade itself is statistically consistent with chance against truth on the followed-up
pool ($\mathrm{AUC}=0.577$ $[0.396,0.757]$; $n=130$ decided, only $8$ refuted): the
\emph{selection} of candidates did the work, and the fine grade adds almost no
incremental truth signal --- so E2 ``parity'' is a low bar concealing a data problem,
not a high one. \emph{Second}, the data to decide small margins does not yet exist:
auditing the Euclid Q1 truth chain (a withdrawn NISP-spectroscopy paper excluded;
SLDE-A's per-object verdicts unpublished) leaves exactly $14$ per-object refutations in
existence across all campaigns, a paired non-inferiority noise floor of ${\sim}0.24$ in
AUC today, falling to $0.078$ only at reader-study scale. \emph{Third}, the machines
nonetheless moved: on the frozen $259$-row gate a Qwen3.5-27B student fine-tuned
directly on the human grade catalog reaches $0.644$ $[0.550,0.733]$ on the hardest
contrast (published A/B lenses vs.\ grade-D human rejects) --- tying the frozen CNN's
$0.646$ $[0.557,0.734]$ and reaching $0.817$ $[0.757,0.873]$ on vetting, a
$+0.11$/$+0.24$ training delta over its zero-shot control --- where v4-style Claude
distillation had failed; and giving a Claude grader the human graders' own information
set produced the program's first statistically significant tier-1 lever
($\dauc=+0.151$ $[+0.020,+0.280]$ over the deployed grader), whose \emph{absolute} level
on the hard contrast then failed to replicate at the gate ($0.487$) --- the
disjoint-draw discipline doing exactly its job. The pre-registered Phase D comparison (paired stratified bootstrap $\dauc$ against the
human grade, DeLong test, non-inferiority margin $\delta_0=0.05$; agreement \qwk{}; an
Euclid-field context arm) then delivers the program's answer: on the $130$
truth-decided candidates, \textbf{both the student ($\mathrm{AUC}=0.685$,
$\dauc=+0.108$ $[-0.028,+0.241]$) and the frozen CNN ($\dauc=+0.182$
$[-0.011,+0.425]$) are formally non-inferior to the expert grade} --- with point
estimates that \emph{favor} the machines, though superiority is not established
(DeLong $p\!\approx\!0.13$) and only eight refuted negatives carry the comparison. The
machines do not reproduce the letter grade itself (\qwk{} $0.044$), and the student's
truth signal does not transfer to the harder Euclid-selected case mix ($0.509$). The
defensible deployment today is therefore score-based vetting through calibrated
operating points --- the letter grades stay human.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question, made decidable}\label{sec:question}
The DESI Legacy Surveys lens search publishes candidates as expert A/B/C grades on
neural-network recommendations \citep{huang2020decals,huang2021desi,storfer2024dr9,
inchausti2025}; the prospect that an automated grader could stand in for the expert
step --- ``as good as a human grader'' --- has hung over the program since \lj{}
Part~I\footnote{\emph{\lj{}: An Agentic Strong-Lens Candidate Grader}, companion
report, \url{https://usfcs-edu.github.io/agentic-lensing/current/lensjudge/}.} measured
a Claude-agent grader at $\kappa\approx0$ against the consensus. That phrase is empty
until two things exist: a measured human level, and a comparison design that could
falsify the claim. The parity program (approved plan, July 2026) supplies both, in four
phases. \textbf{Phase A} measured the human baseline from published data alone ---
condensed in \S\ref{sec:baseline}, derived in full in the companion
report.\footnote{\emph{The Human Baseline for Strong-Lens Grading}, companion report,
\url{https://usfcs-edu.github.io/agentic-lensing/current/lensjudge-human-baseline/}\label{fn:baseline}}
\textbf{Phase B} built a truth-anchored bench and did the power arithmetic
(\S\ref{sec:bench}). \textbf{Phase C} trained and froze the machine systems behind a
leakage firewall and scored each \emph{once} on a frozen gate (\S\ref{sec:machines}).
\textbf{Phase D} is the pre-registered parity comparison itself (\S\ref{sec:phased}).

Two endpoints, fixed before any machine was scored, carry the claim. \textbf{E2
(primary, truth-referenced):} on candidates whose follow-up is decided, compare the
machine score and the human ordinal grade as \emph{paired} predictors of
confirmed-vs-refuted truth, with a non-inferiority margin. Truth --- spectroscopy and
space-resolution imaging --- is the referee, not the humans being compared against.
\textbf{E1 (secondary, agreement):} \qwk{} of machine grades against the published
consensus, read against the measured human anchors. E2 is primary by design: agreement
with a consensus that \S\ref{sec:power} shows is itself nearly uninformative about
truth on this pool would be a strange thing to optimize.

Discipline mattered as much as design. Every training pool was firewalled against the
bench by name and position (\S\ref{sec:pool}); model and operating-point selection
happened only on a selection split (\texttt{valsel}); the frozen gate was touched
exactly once per frozen system; and Phase D's predictors and analysis code were fixed
before the bench was scored. The one selection-vs-gate non-replication the program
produced (\S\ref{sec:cthree}) is reported as prominently as the wins, because it is the
discipline visibly working.

\section{The human baseline, in brief}\label{sec:baseline}
The companion report\textsuperscript{\ref{fn:baseline}} recovered the baseline from
published data alone; Table~\ref{tab:anchors} collects the numbers this report scores
machines against. \emph{Reliability:} the two \citet{huang2021desi} graders gave the
same $1$--$4$ score on only $55.4\%$ of accepted candidates, a mutual $\qwk=0.42$
$[0.37,0.47]$; an individual grader matches the published consensus at $\qwk=0.776$
$[0.757,0.793]$ (an upper bound --- each grader is half the consensus); independent
expert teams grading the same candidates land at $\qwk=0.288$ $[0.121,0.444]$ (SuGOHI
committee, $n=103$; \citealt{sonnenfeld2018sugohi}) and $0.165$ (Euclid Q1 panel,
$n=24$; \citealt{euclidq1engine}) --- consistent with the controlled-experiment
literature \citep{rojas2023vi,canameras2021holismokes}. \emph{Validity:} across every
published per-target follow-up campaign \citep{huang2025foundry1,huang2025foundry2,
agarwal2025foundry3,lin2025foundry4,tran2022agel,barone2025agel,shu2025hscdesi,
sonnenfeld2018sugohi}, $162$ graded candidates have follow-up and $130$ are decided;
confirmation purity is $79/83=95.2\%$ (A), $23/25=92.0\%$ (B), $20/22=90.9\%$ (C) ---
high everywhere, flat in grade, and pointedly not $100\%$ for grade A: four grade-A
candidates are spectroscopically refuted by VLT/MUSE \citep{lin2025foundry4}, every one
an arc-shaped feature at a disqualifying redshift --- the morphology-only failure mode
that grades assigned from $1.3''$-seeing pixels cannot see.

\section{The truth-anchored parity bench}\label{sec:bench}

\subsection{Two arms}\label{sec:arms}
The bench (\texttt{eval/build\_parity\_bench.py}, seed $2026$, SHA-pinned CSVs) has two
arms, matching the two things a parity claim needs: a \emph{paired} human-vs-machine
comparison, and behavior at survey scale.

\textbf{Arm 1 (paired, DESI resolution)} is every truth-followed graded candidate: the
$162$ candidates with published follow-up ($99$~A, $30$~B, $33$~C), of which $130$ are
decided --- $122$ confirmed lenses and $8$ spectroscopic refutations. To these we add
$750$ non-truth augmentation rows ($250$ grade-D human rejects, $500$ random galaxies)
so that operating points and score distributions are estimated in a realistic
candidate mix; augmentation rows never enter a truth endpoint. Total: $912$ rows.

\textbf{Arm 2 (machine at scale, Euclid field)} is all $2{,}584$ Euclid Q1 Strong
Lensing Discovery Engine (SLDE-A) catalog positions \citep{euclidq1engine} served as DESI
Legacy Surveys DR10 cutouts \citep{legacysurvey} --- the machine grades ground-based
pixels of candidates an independent $\sim$10-expert panel graded at $0.1''$
($309$~A, $267$~B, $2{,}008$~C, with the panel's \texttt{expert\_score} and vote counts
carried) --- plus $21$ decided SLDE-B objects outside the Q1 catalog, for $2{,}605$
rows. Of these, $44$ carry per-object truth ($38$ lens, $6$ refuted; modeling tier ---
see \S\ref{sec:integrity}). Cutout staging is complete on both arms ($162/162$;
$2{,}605/2{,}605$).

\subsection{Euclid truth integrity: two findings that shrank the truth set}
\label{sec:integrity}
The plan projected a few hundred Euclid-side adjudications. Auditing the actual truth
chain reduced that to $44$, for two reasons we record because they change what any
group can currently claim. \textbf{(1) The ``NISP spectroscopy of Q1 lenses'' paper is
withdrawn.} The single-author paper offering deflector redshifts for Q1
candidates\footnote{arXiv:2604.02726, withdrawn by the author at v6. Of its $440$
``deflector redshifts,'' $385$ are photometric; its own v5 validation reports $35\%$
blind deflector-$z$ recovery; its per-object catalog was never properly released. Our
extraction (\texttt{parity/data/external/euclid\_q1\_nisp.csv}, $473$ rows, each row's
\texttt{source\_ref} carrying the warning) is retained for reference only and is
deliberately not consumed by the bench builder. A deep-research brief's ``$178$ NISP
$z$-pairs'' traced to this paper's v1 and should be treated as unsupported.} cannot
anchor a truth endpoint. \textbf{(2) SLDE-A's per-object verdicts are unpublished.} The
discovery-engine paper reports only aggregates ($374$ modeled, $315$ judged lens, $59$
judged non-lens), and the Zenodo release's \texttt{unsuccess.zip} mixes modeling
failures, non-lenses, and ambiguous cases with no verdict field
\citep{euclidq1engine,euclidq1data}. Only SLDE-B \citep{euclidq1sldeB} publishes
per-object verdicts: $38$ systems confirmed by automated modeling plus expert
inspection, $6$ ruled out, $11$ model-failed (inconclusive). The recoverable per-object
Euclid truth today is therefore $44$ decided systems, not ${\sim}390$ --- and the
program-wide ledger of per-object \emph{refutations}, the currency of any
truth-referenced comparison, stands at $8$ (arm-1 spectroscopic) $+\,6$ (SLDE-B
modeling) $=14$, anywhere in the literature.

\subsection{Power: what today's truth can decide}\label{sec:power}
Before scoring any machine we asked what the bench can decide
(\texttt{parity/power\_check.py}). The headline reframes the whole program:
\textbf{the human ordinal grade, scored against confirmed-vs-refuted truth on the $130$
decided candidates, has $\mathrm{AUC}=0.577$ $[0.396,0.757]$ --- statistically
consistent with chance.} High purity in every grade plus flatness across grades means
the \emph{selection} of candidates for publication and follow-up did the
discriminative work; conditional on being followed up, the fine grade adds almost no
incremental truth signal. (Caveats: curated, best-first follow-up compresses the score
range; the grade has three levels; there are only $8$ negatives.)

The same arithmetic bounds any verdict. Under an equal-AUC binormal model with paired
correlation $\rho=0.6$, today's $122/8$ split gives a paired-\dauc{} standard error of
$0.095$: the smallest decidable non-inferiority margin is ${\sim}0.24$ --- coarse
screening only. The ladder to a real verdict: ${\sim}30$ refutations (the scale the
grade-agnostic DESI Secondary Target Program, $2{,}157$ systems, should deliver first;
\citealt{huang2025foundry2}) brings it to $0.13$; an Euclid-era $222/60$ set to $0.10$;
reader-study scale ($250/100$) to $0.078$. \textbf{Estimation yes, small-margin verdict
no} --- and we pre-registered Phase D accordingly (\S\ref{sec:dmethods}): margin
$\delta_0=0.05$ as the reader-study standard, with verdicts reported honestly as
inconclusive whenever the CI cannot exclude it.

As a preview, the power check ran the first real paired E2 comparison, using the
published CNN score as ``today's machine'' ($n=105$ decided rows with both predictors,
$99/6$): human $\mathrm{AUC}=0.460$ $[0.278,0.677]$, CNN $0.641$ $[0.362,0.898]$,
paired $\dauc(\mathrm{CNN}-\mathrm{human})=+0.180$ $[-0.029,+0.419]$. The direction
favors the machine; at six negatives it decides nothing (and the CNN column mixes two
catalogs' scores, a per-catalog calibration caveat carried into Phase D).

\section{Machine systems and the frozen gate}\label{sec:machines}

\subsection{The leak-aware pool and the one-shot gate}\label{sec:pool}
All Phase C training and selection draws on one pool
(\texttt{parity/build\_train\_splits.py}): $69{,}228$ rows ($2{,}657$ graded,
$2{,}313$ grade-D human rejects, $64{,}507$ random galaxies) \emph{after} a firewall
that excluded $801$ rows --- $769$ by bench name (including the entire arm-1
augmentation strata) and $32$ by position within $2''$ of an arm-2 target --- with an
audit asserting zero remaining collisions. Splits (\texttt{train}/\texttt{valsel}/%
\texttt{gate}) are stratified by source $\times$ grade, seed $2026$. The gate --- $259$
rows: $139$ graded (including $79$ A/B lenses), $60$ grade-D, $60$ random --- is the
frozen evidence set, scored once per frozen system and never used for selection. Two
contrasts recur below: \textbf{HARD} (published A/B lenses vs.\ grade-D human rejects
--- the human-reject wall that defeated \lj{} v1--v5) and \textbf{vetting} (A/B vs.\
grade-D $+$ random).

\subsection{C1a --- the frozen CNN, and a score-provenance trap}\label{sec:cnn}
The incumbent machine is the search's own residual-network ensemble. Its gate number
came with a trap we record so nobody refalls into it: graded rows in our tables carry
the \emph{reproduction} ensemble score while grade-D rows carry only the
\emph{published} probability, and mixing the two provenances across classes fabricates
discrimination --- gate HARD $\mathrm{AUC}=0.847$ mismatched vs.\ $\mathbf{0.646}$
$[0.557,0.734]$ like-for-like (published score on both sides). The pool now carries
\texttt{p\_pub} and \texttt{p\_repro} as separate columns. Verdict: the frozen CNN has
real but modest rank signal on the human-judged HARD contrast --- far above \lj{} v1's
${\sim}0.5$, well below deployment grade.

\subsection{C1b --- representation probe: the wall is real for engineered features}
\label{sec:rep}
A trained probe over the program's engineered image features (soft-target logistic
regression, $C=0.01$, isotonic calibration fit on \texttt{valsel}) scores gate HARD
$\mathrm{AUC}=0.425$ $[0.330,0.517]$ --- below chance, as was every point of its
selection grid --- against $0.655$ on the easy A-vs-random contrast and $0.716$ on
A/B/C-vs-random. This confirms, with a properly trained and gated probe, the v1
RepresentationKit result: tier-1 engineered features carry essentially no signal
against human-rejected look-alikes. The probe scored both bench arms blind
($912$ and $2{,}605$ rows) as a floor predictor for Phase D.

\subsection{C2 --- the 27B student: human-label SFT succeeds where distillation
failed}\label{sec:student}
The strongest system is a Qwen3.5-27B vision--language student fine-tuned
\emph{directly on the human grade catalog} --- no Claude distillation, the explicit v4
lesson (a distilled student cannot exceed a teacher that \S\ref{sec:cnn} shows is weak;
the argument does not bind when the teacher is the human catalog itself). Corpus
(\texttt{finetune/build\_corpus\_desi.py}, leakage self-check passing): $12{,}250$
dihedral-augmented training rows, $189$ validation, $1{,}174$ \texttt{valsel} selection
rows. Training transplants the proven v5 run-3 recipe verbatim (bf16 LoRA
$r{=}16/\alpha{=}32$ with the vision tower unfrozen, two epochs, 4-GPU DDP on a
Perlmutter A100-80 node; job 55804993, 6h33m, 11 checkpoints). Selection (job 55848953)
graded \texttt{valsel} with seven plateau checkpoints through the pre-registered
grade-token log-probability score, $\plens=P(A)+P(B)$ at the grade token: a monotone
plateau with \texttt{ckpt-750} best (vetting $0.852$, HARD $0.659$). The one-shot gate
(job 55859082) \textbf{held with no collapse: HARD $0.644$ $[0.550,0.733]$, vetting
$0.817$ $[0.757,0.873]$}, against a zero-shot control (same protocol, base weights) of
$0.530$ $[0.430,0.621]$ and $0.576$ --- a training delta of $+0.11$ HARD and $+0.24$
vetting. Direct human-label SFT moves the tier-1 needle that v4's Claude distillation
could not.

\subsection{C3 --- the matched-inputs Claude grader: a real lever, and an honest
non-replication}\label{sec:cthree}
The human-baseline report ends by noting an input asymmetry: the graders saw multi-band
composites with viewer context and redshift metadata, while \lj{}'s deployed grader saw
three rendered views of a bare $101$-px cutout. C3 removes the asymmetry
(\texttt{imaging/grader\_matched.py}; rubric \texttt{prompts/rubric\_matched.md} = the
five published grading criteria verbatim, in a relative-triage framing): full and zoom
composites, a per-band $g\,|\,r\,|\,z$ montage, a $401$-px ($\sim$$105''$) wide-context
view, and a metadata block (Tractor morphology gloss, region, the CNN score,
spectroscopic/photometric redshifts where published, aperture $grz$ magnitudes) ---
while \emph{dropping} the machine-only affordances humans lacked (the residual view and
the aperture-color JSON). The grader is Claude Sonnet~5 on the Agent SDK
\citep{claudeagentsdk}, sharing the deployed grader's transport, JSON contract, and
trace format.

On the $149$-row selection slice ($29$A/$30$B/$30$C/$40$D/$20$ random; both arms
Sonnet~5), matched inputs beat the deployed direct grader decisively: HARD $0.623$
$[0.518,0.725]$ vs.\ $0.436$ $[0.312,0.553]$, paired
$\dauc=\mathbf{+0.151}$ $[+0.020,+0.280]$; on the full mix $0.640$ vs.\ $0.401$,
$\dauc=+0.161$ $[+0.040,+0.281]$. This is \textbf{the first statistically significant
tier-1 lever in the program} --- v1--v5 tried rubrics, agency, reasoning budgets, and
representations and found none --- and it says the missing ingredient was the human
graders' information set. (Robustness: matched graded $149/149$ rows; direct only
$115/149$. Cost ${\sim}\$6$; a live six-candidate smoke first, $\$0.121$.)

The gate said something harder. With the selection frozen --- matched mode, Sonnet~5,
and a \texttt{valsel}-fit operating point ($\plens>0.050$ at target
$\mathrm{FPR}=0.20$, plus an isotonic map, persisted as
\texttt{outputs/c3\_matched\_op\_point.json}) --- the one-shot gate run
($258/259$ rows, $\$4.36$) scored \textbf{HARD $0.487$ $[0.395,0.577]$}: the
\texttt{valsel} HARD level ($0.623$) \emph{did not replicate}. That is selection
optimism on a $149$-row slice, and the disjoint-draw rule caught it --- exactly what
the discipline is for. The broad contrast held ($0.633$ $[0.566,0.699]$,
A/B/C-vs-D$+$random), the frozen operating point ran hot on gate grade-D
($\mathrm{FPR}=0.35$ vs.\ the $0.20$ target), and raw grades stayed cold
($\qwk=0.025$; mean \plens{} on true grade-A $0.09$ --- the score ranks, the labels
don't). Standing conclusion: C3 is a real lens-vs-random \emph{vetter}, not a
HARD-contrast detector; the matched-vs-direct \emph{relative} gain stands (it is
paired, same rows, CI excluding zero), while the absolute HARD level is chance. Two
caveats carry into Phase D: the metadata block includes the CNN score (fair --- the

\section{Phase D: the parity comparison}\label{sec:phased}

\subsection{Pre-registered methods}\label{sec:dmethods}
Everything in this subsection was fixed --- predictors, code
(\texttt{parity/phase\_d\_analysis.py}), seed, margin --- before the bench parquets
were scored; the analysis is a single scored run.

\paragraph{E2, primary.} On the $130$ decided arm-1 rows ($122$ lens, $8$ refuted),
each machine score is compared to the human ordinal grade (A$\,{=}\,3$, B$\,{=}\,2$,
C$\,{=}\,1$) as a paired predictor of truth. For each machine:
$\dauc=\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{machine}}-\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{human}}$ with a paired,
class-stratified bootstrap ($2{,}000$ resamples, seed $2026$) percentile CI and a
two-sided DeLong $p$. Non-inferiority margin $\delta_0=0.05$ (the reader-study
standard): \emph{non-inferior} if the CI lower bound exceeds $-\delta_0$,
\emph{inferior} if the upper bound is below it, \emph{inconclusive} otherwise.
\S\ref{sec:power} says in advance that at $8$ negatives most honest outcomes are
estimation plus ``inconclusive'' at this margin; we report estimation regardless, and
the verdict column is read jointly with the $0.24$ noise floor.

\paragraph{E1, secondary.} \qwk{} between the student's emitted letter grade and the
published consensus on the arm-1 graded rows, read against the measured anchors
($0.776$ team-member upper bound / $0.42$ mutual / $0.29$ independent team).

\paragraph{Arm 2, context.} Truth-referenced AUC on the $44$ SLDE-B-decided rows
($38/6$) for the machine predictors; the Euclid panel's \texttt{expert\_score} scored
on the same rows as a \emph{resolution-privileged upper anchor} --- it saw $0.1''$
pixels, so it is context, not a paired comparator; and grade-stratified student-score
distributions across all $2{,}605$ Euclid-field rows, the at-scale behavioral check.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What ``parity'' can mean when the comparator is at chance.} The most
consequential number in this program may be Table~\ref{tab:anchors}'s last row: on the
pool where truth exists, the human grade discriminates confirmed from refuted at an AUC
statistically consistent with $0.5$. E2 non-inferiority to that comparator is
therefore a \emph{low} bar --- a machine could clear it while being useless --- and a
machine that clears it has \emph{not} thereby shown it ``grades like a trained human.''
What the humans demonstrably did well is upstream of the grade: selecting, from
millions of galaxies, a candidate list whose decided follow-ups confirm at
${\gtrsim}90\%$ in every grade. The honest reading of any Phase D non-inferiority
verdict is symmetric and modest: on curated follow-up targets, the machine's score
carries at least as much (little) incremental truth signal as the expert fine grade.
The agreement endpoint E1, with its measured $0.29/0.42/0.776$ ladder, remains the
harder and more human-flavored target --- and every machine system to date fails it
badly.

\paragraph{The refuted-negative bottleneck.} Fourteen per-object refutations exist,
anywhere, for this task --- eight spectroscopic in the program's own follow-up, six by
Euclid SLDE-B modeling. That single number sets the $0.24$ margin floor, makes every
CI in \S\ref{sec:dresults} wide, and explains why the two Euclid integrity findings
(\S\ref{sec:integrity}) matter beyond bookkeeping: apparent truth that dissolves under
audit (a withdrawn paper, unpublished verdicts) would have manufactured false
statistical power. The path to a real verdict is already scheduled science: the
grade-agnostic DESI Secondary Target Program ($2{,}157$ systems, selection-clean by
construction) and Euclid-Collaboration per-object adjudications at DR1 scale would
bring the decidable margin to $0.13$--$0.078$ --- reader-study territory. The bench,
firewall, and pre-registered analysis here are built to be re-run unchanged on that
data.

\paragraph{Honest scope.} Everything above is conditional on a curated pool:
follow-up campaigns chose their best targets, so the decided set inherits each
campaign's selection function and compresses the score range on which both human and
machine are compared; purities are upper bounds. The comparison is DESI-resolution
only --- the companion Part~I result (grade C largely re-grades to A/B at $0.1''$)
says resolution, not judgment, dominates at the margins. The human predictor is a
three-level ordinal, structurally disadvantaged against continuous scores in AUC
(a granularity, not a competence, artifact --- one more reason E2 verdicts should not
be over-read). Arm-2 truth is modeling-plus-expert, not spectroscopic, and its
refutations are SLDE-B grade-B systems --- a different, easier negative class than
arm 1's spectroscopically dissolved mimics. And the student's training pool, however
firewalled, is drawn from the same survey and grading program as the bench: this is
parity \emph{within} the program's distribution, not a transfer claim.

\paragraph{What to deploy.} The program's recommendation is unchanged by whichever way
the pending verdicts land, because it follows from the structure of the results.
Machine scores are now good enough to \emph{vet at scale} --- the student-27B's
$0.817$ vetting AUC through a calibrated, FPR-matched operating point (never a raw
grade: C3's hot operating point and cold labels, and the residual A/B before it, are
two independent demonstrations) --- so the defensible pipeline is CNN $\to$ student
score for triage and ranking, with expert graders spending their attention on the
survivors. The A/B/C letter grades stay human: no system approaches the E1 ladder, and
the grade's published meaning --- two named experts' consensus --- is not something a
score distribution can impersonate. ``Human parity,'' if the phrase is used at all,
should be used the way this report uses it: as a pre-registered, truth-referenced,
margin-explicit statement about a specific pool, with the selection-vs-gate discipline
that caught our own best lever inflating itself.

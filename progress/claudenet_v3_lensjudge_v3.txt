ClaudeNet — From Engineered Ensemble Diversity to Contaminant-Aware Strong-Lens Finding and Euclid Cross-Validation in the DESI Legacy Surveys. This unified report consolidates three generations of an ML strong-lens finder and the lesson each taught. v1 establishes that architecture is not the bottleneck — a 194K-parameter shielded ResNet ties a 20.5M EfficientNetV2-S to within ±0.003 AUC and the published Inchausti meta-learner collapses to a simple average on correlated bases — so the real lever is member diversity: a deliberately decorrelated ensemble (Spearman ~0.45 vs the lineage's ~1.0) beats the published meta-learner at all four matched-FPR operating points (+0.030/+0.090 vs Storfer at 1%/0.1% FPR; +0.012/+0.090 vs Inchausti), and adds certified conformal-FDR selection, uncertainty triage (selective error 0.022→0.0002 at 50% coverage), and a free +0.04 from test-time equivariance. v2 rebuilds the measuring stick at deployment scale (a 10^6-negative held-out pool) and runs a 17.3M-galaxy DR9 sky sweep yielding 737 FDR-controlled candidates — but a qualification campaign grades zero of the 601 genuinely-new ones as A/B while correctly re-discovering catalogued lenses, decisively diagnosing the binding constraint as lens-vs-mimic separation and vetting resolution, not architecture. v3 answers with a contaminant-aware finder (a typed lens-mimic bank, mimic-blended hard negatives, a recovery@matched-mimic-FPR metric, and a lens-vs-mimic head) that cleanly rejects common mimics (broad-population recovery 0.60→0.89) yet, on an independent Euclid-graded sample, ties the original models at the hardest real-vs-mimic distinction — showing the discovery frontier is resolution-bounded at DECaLS 0.262"/px; cross-validating the DESI∩Euclid-Q1 overlap (DESI pre-screens, Euclid confirms at 0.1", median p_lens 0.03→0.70) yields 49 cross-validated candidates. The honest takeaway: the model lever is real but modest, and the decisive levers for net-new discovery are higher-resolution vetting (Euclid pre-screening) and instrument-native retraining.

LensJudge — An Agentic Visual-Inspection Grader for Strong-Lens Candidates on the Claude Agent SDK. This three-part report builds an LLM-agent VI grader to automate the human bottleneck of turning the CNN finders' thousands of above-threshold cutouts into A/B/C/D grades, and reports three generations. Part I (v1) returns a sharp, load-bearing negative: the agent is strong where the task has clean physical features and confirmed labels (the spectroscopic channel — 20/20 Hsu Table-2 Grade-A pairs plausible, 4/4 Foundry-II non-lenses rejected), but on imaging grading of the CNN's hard high-score pool no configuration (lean Sonnet/Opus, judge panel, multi-agent, even a GIGA-Lens fit or hand-engineered representations) beats chance against single-consensus A/B/C labels (AUC 0.32–0.51); the wall is ground-based resolution and soft labels, not the grader — so the prescription is to deploy as a high-recall detect/escalate pre-filter. Part II (v2) builds exactly that: a contaminant-aware rubric targeting the dominant LRG+companion/ring/spiral/blend mimics lifts lens-vs-mimic ranking from chance to AUC 0.71 (+0.20) while detection barely moves; a live-validated two-tier DESI→Euclid 0.1" escalation flips 140 ambiguous cross-matches from undecided to confirmed (median p_lens 0.03→0.70, ~90% agreement with Euclid experts); and an agency ablation shows the agentic loop is unnecessary for detection (a loop-free grader matches it ~6× cheaper) but essential for mimic adjudication, motivating a cost-aware cascade (~$170 per 5k survivors). Part III (v3) deploys it and widens the resolution lever: an HSC PDR3 0.6" tier-2 recovers 92% of the SuGOHI lenses DESI buries (median p_lens 0.04→0.62), a rank-routed cascade (stage-1 detection AUC 0.605) routes only survivors to the expensive agentic loop, and an active-learning loop exports the agent's confirmed mimics back to the finder as hard negatives. It is explicit about what stays unsolved — the gains are in ranking rather than tight-FPR recovery, the fine A/B/C grade remains irreducibly soft even at 0.1", and the escalated objects are cross-validated catalogue lenses, not new discoveries.
